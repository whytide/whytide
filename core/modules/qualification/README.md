# qualification

Decides which persons become qualified leads. Three stages are here: the hard filters, pure code that stops a person whose title, country or company size cannot match the ICP before any model sees them (alpha-p6-010); the assessment, where the agent `qualification.assess-fit` judges an anonymized person against the ICP's six criteria and the verdicts are stored as a snapshot (alpha-p6-020); and the score, where code computes the fit, the status, the tier and the why from those verdicts and gives the lead its decision (alpha-p6-030). The batch runs the three per lead, with the credits, in the worker, and the re-score scores the scored leads again after a criteria save (alpha-p6-040). On the lead page, the module's panel re-scores a lead and says what comes next for one not scored (alpha-p10-100). The golden set exports the reviewed leads without names, for the evals (alpha-p9-050).

- **Owns:** `score_snapshots`, `criterion_verdicts` and `assessment_attempts` in `core/packages/db/prisma/schema/qualification.prisma` ([docs/data.md](docs/data.md)); the rules in `domain/hard-filters/`; the anonymizer in `domain/anonymize/`; the agent in `agents/assess-fit/` ([its README](agents/assess-fit/README.md)); the scorer in `domain/score/` and the why in `domain/why/`, whose message keys it owns (`ui/labels.ts`); the batch and its tick in `workflows/`; the re-score in `domain/rescore.ts`, its route in `api/` and its command in `cli/`; the snapshot route (alpha-p6-060); the golden set in `domain/export/`, its route and its command, and the why bullets' source of the grounding sample there; the Re-score panel in `ui/rescore-action/` ([Screen](#screen)).
- **Public interface:** `index.ts`, below.
- **Depends on:** `ai` (callAgent, `rawPrompts`, `defineGroundingSource`), `contacts` (`getPerson`, `getCompany`, `getLead`, `listLeads`, `applyQualification`, `leadsPendingQualification`, `latestReviews`), `icp` (`readIcpForScoring`, `icp.updated`), `discovery` (`discovery.people_imported`), `tenancy` (`listWorkspaceIds`, `findWorkspaceBySlug`), `db`, `platform` (the credits client, `startWorkflow`), `providers` (the files port); `@nestjs/common` in `api/` only, whose snapshot route takes contacts' `leads.read` and `lead_not_found`, and the golden set's `exports.read`; `@whytide/ui`, the web's API client, `next`, `next-intl` and `react` in `ui/`, which the web host compiles.
- **Reviewers:** the owners of this module; two tables hold personal data, and the anonymizer decides what a model sees ([docs/security.md](docs/security.md)).

## The batch

`workflows/` (alpha-p6-040; [docs/flows.md](docs/flows.md)). The global schedule `qualification.batch_tick`, every `QUALIFICATION_SCHEDULE_MINUTES`, overlap skip, runs `qualification_batch_tick`, whose activity lists the workspaces through tenancy's `listWorkspaceIds` and starts the `qualification_batch` of each whose ICP has targeting (one never saved has none) and a lead to assess, checked in the workspace's own run; a workspace that fails leaves the others started, and the tick then fails with its code. The subscribers of `discovery.people_imported` and `icp.updated` start it at once, for the workspace of the delivery. Every start is `batchStart`: one run of `qualification.batch:<workspaceId>:current` at a time (`startWorkflow`'s `oneAtATime`), so a start while one runs starts none and the next tick picks up what it would have read.

A batch reads up to `QUALIFICATION_BATCH_SIZE` leads, oldest first, `pending` or decided at an older ICP version (contacts' `leadsPendingQualification`). Each step is an activity of its own, and no tenant run is held across the model call:

1. `qualification_filter_leads`, one run: the ICP read and its filters prepared once; a miss is `hard_filtered` with the reason, the why bullet `qualification.why.hard_filtered` (the compared values) and the ICP version, no credits.
2. `qualification_reserve`: `credits.reserve` of `qualification.assessment`, key `qual:{personId}:{icpVersion}:{model}:{promptVersion}`, with `:{attempt}` from the lead's second attempt at that version (`assessment_attempts`), lease `async`. `insufficient_credits` leaves a `pending` lead with that `status_reason`, and a lead decided at an older ICP version as it was, and stops the batch; the next batch tries it again.
3. `qualification_assess` (kind `ai`): `assessPerson`, outside any run. It answers ids and codes only.
4. `qualification_commit`, one run: `scoreSnapshot`, then `credits.commit`. A reservation past its 24-hour lease is released and the lead's attempt moved; the batch reserves again under the next attempt's key and commits. On `ai_failed`, `qualification_release` releases and writes the lead `ai_failed` with the reason (`invalid`, `gates`, `empty`, `length`, `content_filter`).

Every release moves the lead's attempt, so a released key is never reserved again. A provider failure (an `llm_*` code) past the activity's attempts releases and stops the batch: the lead waits `pending` with `provider_failed` for a later batch, and its third attempt at the version is `ai_failed` with `provider_failed`. Any other failure of the assessment (`assessment_agent_not_loaded`, a lasting database error) fails the batch and keeps the reservation, which the next batch's reserve finds by its key. An ICP that moved during the batch releases, leaves the lead and stops it. A failed commit fails the batch and keeps the reservation too. A cancelled batch waits for its running step's outcome and releases an assessment it stopped. Every decided lead publishes `qualification.lead_qualified` in the run that wrote it.

## The re-score

A criteria save (weights, must, enabled) moves no ICP version, so the batch selects nothing (decisions.md, 2026-10-22). `POST …/qualification/rescore` (`@Idempotent()`, `qualification.rescore`; body `{}` or `{ personIds }`, 1 to 500) and `pnpm rescore --workspace <id or slug> [--person <id>]` write `qualification.rescore_requested`; its subscriber starts `qualification_rescore`, one run per request. The route answers 202 `{ requestId, marked }`: `marked` counts the leads the run would move now, by the rule below, read in the request's run. The run takes the named persons, or every person with an assessment at the current ICP version, by id, in pages of 50, each page one activity and one run (`rescorePage`). A lead whose current snapshot is scored at the current version by other criteria (`scored_criteria_hash`, [docs/data.md](docs/data.md)) is scored again from its verdicts with the current criteria: a copy of the snapshot keyed with the criteria's hash (`criteria_hash`), scored by `scoreSnapshot`, takes the lead; the snapshot these criteria scored before, a copy or the assessment's own, takes it back. Each publishes `qualification.lead_qualified`. No model call and no credits. Skipped: no lead, no scored snapshot at the current version (the batch assesses it), or criteria that scored it already.

## The golden set

The reviewed leads as the evals read them, without names (alpha-p9-050; RB-25.4.1). `GET …/qualification/exports/golden-set.jsonl[?week]`, under contacts' `exports.read`, and `pnpm export:golden --workspace <id or slug> [--week <ISO week>]` build it alike: a line per lead with a decision, or with `week` per lead whose latest decision falls in that ISO week (contacts' `latestReviews`), by person id. The file goes through the files port to `exports/golden-set-<date>.jsonl` (UTC; kept 7 days at most; a second export that day replaces it; 25 MiB at most, `files_too_large`). The route answers it (`application/jsonl`); the command prints `golden_set_written: w/<workspaceId>/exports/golden-set-<date>.jsonl, <n> lines`.

A line ([docs/data.md](docs/data.md)) holds the snapshot the lead had at its decision: the assessment's input, the verdicts, the disqualifiers, the status, the fit and the tier; then the decision, its reason code and the tester's texts; the person is the sha256 of its id. The input is read back from the decision record's `raw_prompt` until its `raw_purge_after` (`input_from: "decision_record"`); after that time, purged or not, or for a lead decided before any assessment, the anonymizer rebuilds it from the person, the company and the ICP as they are now, without evidence (`"rebuilt"`).

## Screen

A panel in the `actions` slot of contacts' lead page (`ui/nav.ts` `panels`; `ui/rescore-action/`): `panel.tsx` reads the lead on the server, as the member, through contacts' lead route; `rescore-action.tsx` shows it in the browser. A lead with no scored snapshot shows a short note instead, which says what comes next: `qualification.explain.pending` (by its `status_reason`: none, `insufficient_credits`, `provider_failed`), `qualification.explain.ai_failed` (`provider_failed`, or the model's answer), else `qualification.action.empty`. A scored lead has Re-score (`qualification.action.rescore`), `qualification.explain.needs_review` under it when it needs review. Re-score posts `{ personIds: [personId] }`; while the request runs and, when it marked the lead, until a new snapshot takes the lead (asked every two seconds, thirty seconds at most), the button is busy and the kit's loading state shows. Then `qualification.action.rescored` with the new status, and the page reads its data again; `qualification.action.unchanged` when the request marked none; `qualification.action.waiting` after thirty seconds. A failed read or request is the kit's error state, the code in words, whose retry reads the page again or asks again. Its specs are the web host's `tests/qualification-states.spec.ts` and `tests/rescore-action.spec.ts`.

## Decided here

- An ICP title matches when the person's title holds every one of its tokens; a role collapses to one token (vice president is `vp`, so a vp is no president), a function's abbreviation expands to its words (`sdr`). The task's `head of` = `director of` is read as director = head wherever it stands.
- Unknown, so passing: no title or one of noise only, no country, no company row (the task's Open question), a size that reads as no count. An ICP list left empty filters nothing, and an ICP title of noise only is skipped.
- The order is title, country, size; the first miss is the result.
- A size spanning several of icp's ranges (`1-50`) passes when one of them is in the ICP's.
- `domain/hard-filters/size.ts` repeats icp's closed list of ranges with their bounds; the type-level test fails if icp's list gains a range this one lacks.
- The ICP's criteria (enabled, must) do not switch a filter off: its lists alone decide.
- After review: `prepareHardFilters(icp)` reads the ICP's titles once, since reading them on every call missed the 50 ms at fifty titles. No memo either: it would be state shared by every workspace of the process.
- The assessment (alpha-p6-020) takes the batch activity's context rather than opening runs of its own: callAgent opens its own runs and must run outside any, and a run inside the activity's would be `nested_run`.
- A snapshot per decision record and ICP version (a unique key): a retried activity, which the record's cache answers without a model call, gets the stored snapshot back.
- The table names the task gives; the snapshot also keeps `disqualifiers`, which the score (alpha-p6-030) and the lead page (alpha-p6-060) read, and its status, confidence and tier are enums now, so alpha-p6-030 needs no migration.
- The success result carries `status: "assessed"`, so that a caller tells it from `ai_failed` by one field.
- The score (alpha-p6-030) reads the ICP's criteria from `readIcpForScoring`, never constants: the weights and the musts are the ICP's rows. So `decide` and `buildWhy` take the criteria too, and `buildWhy` one object, with the hard filters' reason beside their detail.
- A disabled criterion counts for nothing: out of the normalization, no cap and no review as a must, no unknown in the confidence, no bullet; its verdict row keeps weight 0.
- The fit is rounded half up to the column's integer; the cap, the gate and the tier read the rounded fit. When no criterion counts, the fit is 0.
- `needs_review` holds a lead at or above the gate only; below it the lead is `not_qualified`, a must unknown or not. The tier comes from the fit alone, a disqualified lead's too, as the task says.
- Of several disqualifiers, the reason and the bullet name the first in the closed list's order, whatever the model's.
- The why: what stops the lead first (the hard filters' miss, a disqualifier or a must at `not_met`), then the fit (the heaviest criterion met, its quote, how many met), then the gap (the heaviest unknown, how many more). Params are codes, numbers and quotes, a quote cut to 300 code points ending with "…"; a message words a code through an ICU `select`.
- A lead the hard filters stopped gets `qualification.why.hard_filtered`, not the must-missed key: a filter is no criterion, and the ICP's flags never switch one off.
- `scoreSnapshot` runs in the caller's run, so the batch can commit its credits in the same transaction. A scored snapshot is final: a retry gets the stored result. A lead never moves back to a snapshot created before its current one (ties by id). A snapshot of another ICP version is `icp_version_stale`. A criteria save re-scores nothing (decisions.md): the re-score from stored verdicts is alpha-p6-040's.
- A verdict row's `weight` is its criterion's weight in this fit and `points` the verdict's (1, 0.5, 0.4, 0), so the rows give the fit before the cap.
- `ui/labels.ts` writes each key out in a `t()` call, so that the message check reads it; contacts' screens read a why bullet's key as data.
- From the review of alpha-p6-020: an activity whose snapshot insert loses to a concurrent one on the same decision record (P2002, which aborts its run) reads the stored snapshot in a run of its own.
- The batch (alpha-p6-040): one global tick that starts a batch per workspace (decisions.md), no `rescore_pending` status (the batch reads the leads decided at an older ICP version), and `qualification.lead_qualified` for the task's `lead.qualified`.
- The hard filters, once per batch, run in one activity with the selection; the reservation, the assessment and the commit or release are three, since credits' lock lasts the reserving run.
- A lead `hard_filtered` or `ai_failed` keeps the ICP version, so a targeting change reads it again. A provider failure stops the batch, as the provider is likely down for every lead.
- The event carries the lead's status and tier as stored, read after the write; none for a lead left `pending`. A retried activity may publish twice (at least once).
- A `not_qualified` lead gets no tier (decisions.md); the snapshot keeps the computed one.
- The tick lists the workspaces through tenancy's `listWorkspaceIds`: no module reads another's tables, and every global fan-out goes through it (decisions.md, 2026-10-22).
- The re-score (decisions.md, 2026-10-22): `criteria_hash` joins the snapshot's unique key, and the assessment's own snapshot keeps it empty. Each lead re-scored publishes `qualification.lead_qualified`, as the batch does: its decision may have changed.
- alpha-p10-100: every snapshot stores the hash of the criteria that scored it, `scored_criteria_hash`, outside the unique key, so a re-score with unchanged criteria writes no copy, and the criteria that scored the assessment give its own snapshot back. The migration fills it for the copies; an assessment's own snapshot scored before it stays empty, so its first re-score still writes one copy.
- alpha-p10-100: the lead's attempt (`assessment_attempts`, one row per person and ICP version, none for the first) moves at every release: a provider failure, an `ai_failed` answer, an ICP that moved, a cancelled step or a lease run out. So a released key is never reserved again, and the first attempt's key keeps its old form, which a reservation held across the deploy is found by. A failure that is not the provider's keeps the reservation, so a lasting one holds a lead's credits until the lease frees them, then the next batch reserves again.
- alpha-p10-100, from the coordinator: the batch's and the re-score's activities wait for their cancellation to complete (`ActivityCancellationType.WAIT_CANCELLATION_COMPLETED`, as signals' run), so a cancelled batch's step finishes and the batch settles or releases what it reserved.
- alpha-p10-100: an ICP "never saved" is one with no targeting (no list, must-haves or disqualifiers): icp keeps no saved flag, and such an ICP filters nothing and tells the model nothing. `startBatches` tries every workspace, then fails with the first failure, so the tick shows it.
- alpha-p10-100: `marked` applies the run's rule in the request's run, each named person through contacts' `getLead`, or every lead through `listLeads` by pages of 100; the run, later, may differ if the criteria or the leads change between. The panel reads the lead through contacts' route over HTTP, as the page does, and polls the snapshot route; its keys are snake_case as the module's others (`explain.needs_review`, not the task's `needsReview`), and its specs are the web host's, as the other screens'.
- The snapshot route (alpha-p6-060) is a read of the lead's `current_score_snapshot_id` through contacts' `getLead`, so a person without a lead, another workspace's included, is contacts' `lead_not_found`, and a lead never scored `score_snapshot_not_found`. It answers no `modelId`, `promptVersion` or decision record: the page shows none, and the record is read in the database. `scoredAt` is the snapshot's `created_at`, written just before it is scored, since the table keeps no other time.
