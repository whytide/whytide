# messaging: concepts

- **Dossier:** everything a draft may say about a lead, in five blocks: the sender, the offer (Company Knowledge), the lead (the person's business fields), the company and why now (the person's signal events with their evidence, newest first). It is built in code from other modules' public reads, never their tables, and is the writer's only source.
- **Fact:** one value of a block, listed once with an id (`f_1`, `f_2`, … in the blocks' order), a kind and a source reference, the record and the column it came from. Equal values from two fields are two facts. The writer cites fact ids; a draft records the ones it used.
- **Claim:** a fact of kind `claim`, a Company Knowledge proof point: the only claims a draft may make. A pain point or the one-liner is a `quote`, wording to reuse, never evidence.
- **Entity:** a fact's value as the grounding gate compares it: normalized (compatibility form, no accents, lower case, single spaces), once per kind, a date as its UTC day. Claims and quotes give none: the gate searches their text itself.
- **Hash:** the SHA-256 of the dossier's canonical JSON. The same reads give the same ids and the same hash; a changed fact changes it, so a draft that keeps it shows when its dossier moved on: the draft panel calls such a draft stale, shows none of its facts, and Regenerate writes it again from today's.
- **Draft:** one first LinkedIn message to a lead on a channel, `linkedin_note` (a connection request's note) or `linkedin_message`, with the plan it was written from, its text, its gates' results, its regenerations and its model calls' records. `drafting` until written, then `ready`, `flagged` (a gate's issue left after two regenerations) or `failed` (a code). A member's own text makes it `edited`, and nothing writes it again; a regeneration is a new draft, the replaced one kept.
- **Plan:** the planner's answer: an angle (`signal_comment`, `signal_reaction`, `pain_point`, `proof_point`), the ids of the facts to use and a call to action (`question`, `soft_ask`, `resource`). The writer writes the text from it.
- **Gate:** a check in code of one validated answer, which answers its issues and never throws: `facts_used` on a plan; `placeholders`, `length`, `grounding`, `red_lines` and `colleague_signal` on a text. An issue names what failed: the fact id, the placeholder, the length and its range, the entity, the phrase. A message's range is 150 to 300 characters, which the panel's counter says; a note's, 1 to `LINKEDIN_NOTE_LIMIT`. `checkText` runs the text gates but grounding on every edit and approval.
- **Regeneration:** the same call again, with every earlier answer's issues in its `<failures>` block; two per draft at most, the plan's and the text's together.
- **Colleague signal:** a text that points at another person of the lead's company: a phrase such as "your colleague" or "someone on your team" (`COLLEAGUE_SIGNAL_PHRASES`, the last word also plural), or a colleague's first and last name together. A colleague's activity is theirs, never the lead's reason to hear from us, so the `colleague_signal` gate refuses it in the writer, in an edit and in an approval.
- **Approved:** a sequence step's draft whose final text the owner approved (`approveStepDraft`), as written or changed. From then on its text is frozen: nothing writes it again.
- **Content hash:** the hex SHA-256 of the exact final text approved, of `""` for an empty note. The send compares the text it is about to send with it.
- **Edit ratio:** how much the owner changed the writer's text before approving it: the Levenshtein distance between the two trimmed texts over the longer length, 0 to 1, rounded to 3 decimals. 0 for a text approved as written, 1 for one typed over a failed draft.
