# messaging.write-email

The email writer (gamma-p3-070), the second call of an email's draft after the shared planner (F09 §5.1): a first email's subject and body, or a follow-up's body, from the dossier and the plan. A third agent rather than a channel of the LinkedIn writer: an email has a subject and limits in words, so its prompt, schema and evals stay apart. [docs/flows.md](../../docs/flows.md) says how a draft is made, [docs/security.md](../../docs/security.md) what the model sees and the gates.

- **Task:** `task.ts`: `kind: generation`, no tools, prompt version 1, the LinkedIn writer's budget (8,000 input tokens, 1,500 output tokens, 0 credits). The model is `AI_MODEL_MESSAGING_WRITE_EMAIL`, else the LLM port's first. The module never imports the file: `writeDraft` takes the task from the agent registry.
- **Prompt:** `prompt.v1.md`. The LinkedIn writer's blocks, `<channel>` an email's: the body's words (`EMAIL_WORD_RANGE`, 50 to 120) and a first email's subject's limits (6 words, 60 characters), null for a follow-up. `<step>` is always given: `email_first`, or `email_follow_up` with the earlier steps' texts, untrusted and no facts. The rules of the LinkedIn writer, plus: a signal named by its topic, never its act; no link, address or domain in a first email; a subject in sentence case with no `Re:`, `Fw:` or `Fwd:`, none on a follow-up, which goes in the first email's thread; and every gate it can meet explained in `<failures>`. Once drafts have run on it, a changed prompt is `prompt.v2.md` with `promptVersion: 2`, and runs the evals.
- **Output:** `subject` (a string for a first email, null for a follow-up) and `body`, 1 to 4,000 characters. The module's gates check them after the call (`domain/gates/`): `placeholders`, `length` (the body's words), `grounding`, `red_lines`, `colleague_signal` and `signal_act` on the subject and the body; `subject`; `links` for a first email; `near_duplicate` for a step's email against its mailbox's drafts of the last 24 hours.
- **Evals:** `core/packages/ai/evals/messaging.write-email/` (gamma-p3-075), twenty cases: ten synthetic leads on the LinkedIn writer's two sellers, a first email and a follow-up each, every `expected` marked `synthetic`, every profile in English. The scorer runs the gates in short on the subject and the body after every answer (the near-duplicate gate needs the mailbox's drafts, which no case has), then checks `subject` (a first email's within `<channel>`'s limits, on one line, with no `Re:`, `Fw:` or `Fwd:`; a follow-up's none), `length` (the body's words), `links` (none in a first email), `grounding` (0.9), `red_lines`, `colleague_signal` (also none of the case's `colleagues`), `signal_act`, `no_repeat` (a follow-up does not open with the first six words of an earlier email), the words the case expects (0.8) and that an injection in a lead's post fails safe; three first answers, an act, a link and a follow-up's subject, are refused and pass on the retry (`pnpm -F ai eval messaging`). It reads the LinkedIn writer's scorer's phrase lists, which `core/packages/ai/tests/evals.test.ts` keeps equal to the module's.
