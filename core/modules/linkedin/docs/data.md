# linkedin: data

Seven tenant tables and three global ones in `core/packages/db/prisma/schema/linkedin.prisma`, created by the migrations `20261022182000_linkedin_sender_accounts`, `20261022191500_linkedin_quota_and_log` (its CHECKs: no negative count, `quota_day` a date) and `20261029111025_linkedin_event_receiver` (its CHECK: a receipt's kind is a person event's; `app` refused UPDATE on the receipts), widened by `20261029105720_linkedin_send_quotas`, `20261029122723_linkedin_send_reservations`, `20261029141856_linkedin_kill_switches`, `20261029163222_linkedin_outreach_send_refs`, `20261029182307_linkedin_sending_disclosure`, `20261029183928_linkedin_health` and `20261029194717_linkedin_pending_invites` (below). Ids are `sac_`, `squ_`, `sqr_`, `sal_`, `sor_`, `ler_`, `lpc_`, `lwc_`, `lce_` and `she_` `<ULID>` (`ids.ts`); times are `timestamptz`.

`linkedin_send_quotas` adds the columns with their defaults (counters and limits 0, targets 5 and 10), lowers every `daily_read_cap` above 100 to 100, gives the existing usage rows their account's cap, at most 100, as the allowance, and then adds the CHECKs, the hard maximums of `domain/quota/constants.ts`: `daily_read_cap` 1 to 100, `invite_target` 5 to 15, `message_target` 10 to 40, `ramp_updated_on` a date; every counter at least 0, `invite_limit` 0 to 25, `message_limit` 0 to 80, `read_allowance` 0 to 100, and the three together at most 185, the 200 less the 15 withdrawals. The counters carry no CHECK against the maximums: an action made is always counted, and the reservations keep the counts inside the limits.

`linkedin_send_reservations` adds `retry_at`, the reservations' type (existing rows `read`), key and note, `committed_late`, the log's send kinds, `send_check` and note, and `time_zone` (existing rows their account's zone, else `UTC`; the trigger `sender_quota_usage_time_zone` gives the same to a row the previous release inserts without it while an upgrade runs). Its CHECKs: a send's reservation carries its key and a count of 1, a read's no key (`sender_quota_reservations_send_key`); the log's `outcome` is one of `ACTION_OUTCOMES` in `schemas/quota.ts` (`sender_action_log_outcome_known`; `tests/action-log.test.ts` keeps the two equal). `REVOKE UPDATE ON sender_action_log FROM app`: `app` inserts, reads and deletes log rows, never updates one.

`linkedin_kill_switches` adds the three controls tables and the account's two columns (existing accounts not paused). Its CHECKs: a stop's source is set exactly when it is on (`sender_accounts`, `linkedin_workspace_controls`), and `singleton` is true. It creates no instance row, so an upgraded instance has sending closed until the operator opens it. `REVOKE DELETE ON linkedin_platform_controls FROM app`, since a deleted row would lift the instance's stop, and `REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE ON linkedin_control_events FROM app`: the events are appended only (42501).

No foreign key to `workspaces`, `users` or `persons`. Personal data: `sender_outreach` names a person by id and by their member id at the vendor, never logged; a kept message's text is in a raw file, `raw/linkedin/messages/<event_key_hash>.json` (refs, no person id), which raw/'s 72 hours expire. The reference names the user's own LinkedIn account at the provider (PII class personal, provenance: the provider's webhook), sealed under `APP_MASTER_KEY` with the organization and the purpose `linkedin_account`. The label is the user's. `status_checked_at` is the last time the webhook or a refresh stored the provider's status; `connected_at` is the owner's last connect, which only the pairing writes. The events, in the platform's outbox, carry ids only: `{ sender_account_id }` for `linkedin.sender_account_connected`, `linkedin.sender_account_disconnected`, `linkedin.status_refresh_requested` and `linkedin.account_replaced`; `linkedin.invite_accepted { sender_account_id, person_id, detected_at }` and `linkedin.message_received { sender_account_id, person_id, direction, file_key }`. A user's disconnect deletes the reference and its hash and keeps the row, its member id's hash included. The deletion script's `erasePerson` deletes a person's outreach rows and the action-log rows whose `object_ref` is their id (privacy's README "Erasers"); the daily job, at 00:30 UTC, deletes the receipts received more than 7 days before (`received_at`, `EVENT_RECEIPT_DAYS`) and the action-log rows of more than 90 days before (`at`, `ACTION_LOG_DAYS`; `domain/retention/constants.ts`), 5000 rows per statement (`PURGE_BATCH_ROWS`); nothing else is deleted. The ramp writes `invite_target`, `message_target` and `ramp_updated_on`, the account's quota day of its last step or restart; the daily job also expires the reservations past their hold of a day no reservation swept, as the sweep does.

`linkedin_outreach_send_refs` (beta-p1-050) adds to `sender_outreach` `last_step_execution_id` (the step execution, `sx_…`, whose send settled on the row last; indexed with `workspace_id`, for `sentMessageRef`) and `last_message_ref` (the vendor's ref of the account's last message to the person, cleared by an invite's or a withdrawal's send), both text and null.

`linkedin_sending_disclosure` adds `disclosure_version` (every existing row 1, so an account connected in alpha sends nothing until its owner accepts; a CHECK of at least 1), `personal_account_confirmed_at`, `member_id_hash` and `member_id_checked_at`, the partial unique index, and `member_id_unknown` and `sender_account_in_use` to the log's outcomes. A receipt written before it holds a plain SHA-256, which a redelivery no longer finds; none exists while no send has gone out, since only an outreach row's events are kept.

`linkedin_health` (beta-p6-010) adds the enums `sender_health_state` (`healthy`, `at_risk`, `restricted`, `checkpoint`, `disconnected`), `sender_health_trigger` (`acceptance_rate`, `rate_limit_errors`, `checkpoint`, `limit_reached`, `session_invalid`, `manual`) and `sender_health_action` (`paused_all_actions`, `paused_invites`, `resumed`); to `sender_accounts` `health_state` (`healthy` for every existing row), `health_changed_at` and `paused_until`, a CHECK keeping `paused_until` null outside `restricted` and `checkpoint`; and the tenant table `sender_health_events`: `workspace_id`, `sender_type` (`linkedin`, a CHECK), `sender_id` (the account), `from_state`, `to_state`, `trigger`, `metrics` (jsonb, numbers and codes only), `automatic_action` (null when none), `paused_until` (the end of the pause the event set), `ack_required`, `acknowledged_by`, `acknowledged_at`, `owner_notified_at` (the owner's mail went), `operator_notified_at` (beta-p6-030's), `created_at`; indexed on `(workspace_id, sender_id, created_at)`. `app` reads, inserts and updates the events, never deletes one: no person data, so no eraser, and the history stays.

`linkedin_pending_invites` (beta-p4-070) adds to `sender_accounts` `pending_invites` (int, null until the account's first check of its pending invitations and again after a replacement, a CHECK of at least 0: the count the check wrote, plus each invite committed since) and `pending_invites_checked_at` (null); to `sender_outreach` `relation_checked_at` (null: when the check asked about an invite the vendor no longer listed and found it accepted or gone, so it is asked once); and the log's kinds `pending_invites` (a page of the list) and `relation_check` (one invite, `object_ref` the person's id). Every existing account starts with no count, so it sends no invite before its first check.

`linkedin_action_log_call_ref` (beta-p6-020) adds `call_ref` (text, null) to `sender_action_log`: the third error counts the distinct calls among the account's `linkedin_rate_limited` and `linkedin_provider_error` rows of the last 60 minutes, a row without one its own call, so the retries of one read or one send count once. Existing rows keep null.
